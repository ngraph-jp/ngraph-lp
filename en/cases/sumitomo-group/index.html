Context

In 1590, Riemon Soga, the business founder, began copper refining and copperwork in Kyoto (Masatomo Sumitomo, the family founder, ran a book and medicine shop in Kyoto). Later moved into mining. The Besshi copper mine (1691–1973) was the operating backbone for 282 years. Even after post-war zaibatsu dissolution, Sumitomo Metal Mining, Sumitomo Chemical, Sumitomo Corporation, and Sumitomo Bank (now SMBC) remain linked. The 'Business Principles' placed at the head of the house code in 1891 (sound management, trust, adapting to change) live on as company precepts.

Teles' Insight

Sumitomo's 436-year core is the managerial constitution called 'Business Principles.' Codified in 1891 from the spirit of founder Masatomo's precepts, 'honor trust, don't chase passing profits' — that document has functioned for 400 years as the decision standard for the banto managers. They ran a single mine, Besshi copper, for 282 years, and expanded into chemicals, metals, and trading before it ran out. A structure where the family owns but delegates management to banto erases generational risk. A single passage written by a founder can unify decisions for hundreds of years — the real power of management philosophy.

Key to survival

A one-sentence managerial constitution — 'don't chase passing profits, honor trust, respond to the times' — has operated as the decision standard for generations of banto managers across 436 years. Splitting ownership from management, with the family owning and the banto running the business, erases generational risk and lets them steer in a single direction for centuries on a single house code.
